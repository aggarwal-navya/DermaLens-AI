The pipeline, the data, how we evaluated it — and, just as important, where it falls short.

01

Pipeline overview

Input

Photo + metadata

Skin photo resized to 288 px; optional age group, sex and body sites.

Gate

Skin check

Photos that do not look like skin are returned as “not skin”, with no diagnosis attempted.

Backbones

Image ensemble

Ensemble of fine-tuned vision backbones (e.g. ConvNeXt-Small + ViT-Base DINOv2).

Fusion

Metadata fusion

Image embeddings combined with encoded metadata; temperature-calibrated softmax.

Output

Decide + explain

Open-set threshold → identified / healthy / not identified; Grad-CAM heatmap.

02

Datasets

Most public dermatology datasets are dominated by lighter skin. DermaLens is trained and tuned on data from Indian and South Asian patients, where inflammation often appears brown, violet or grey rather than red.

DermaCon-IN

Dermatologist-labelled photos from South Indian outpatient clinics (Fitzpatrick types 3–6). Source for all five conditions, including Psoriasis.

SkinDisNet

Smartphone photos from two Bangladeshi medical colleges — Tinea, Scabies, Eczema and Contact Dermatitis. Original images only; augmented copies excluded.

MCSI (healthy class)

A small set of web-collected normal-skin images (CC BY 4.0, credited) used for the Healthy class.

03

Evaluation approach

Patient-grouped split

All photos of one patient stay in the same split, so the test set never contains a familiar face or lesion.

Bootstrap CIs

Metrics are reported with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals, not just a single point estimate.

Fairness by skin type

Accuracy is compared across Fitzpatrick types 3–4 vs 5–6 (DermaCon-IN subset) to check darker skin is not under-served.

Shortcut test

Same-disease, different-source tests check whether the model recognises the disease or merely the dataset’s photo style.

04

Metrics

Held-out, patient-grouped test set. Values load from the deployed model card.

Model test-set metrics
MetricValueWhat it means
Accuracy—Share of test photos classified correctly.
Macro F1—Average F1 across classes; rare classes count equally.
Balanced accuracy—Mean per-class recall; robust to class imbalance.
Open-set AUROC—How well unseen conditions are separated from known ones (1.0 = perfect).
Test images—Photos in the held-out test set.

05

Limitations

  • Covers only five conditions plus healthy skin; anything else should be rejected as “not identified”, but rejection is not perfect. Not for skin cancer.
  • Healthy and Psoriasis each come from a single dataset, so scores may partly reflect dataset style.
  • Fairness results cover DermaCon-IN only; Fitzpatrick type 6 has very few test images. North Indian populations are not directly sampled.
  • Trained on clinical photos; dermoscopy images are out of scope.
  • Photo quality (lighting, blur, distance, camera) strongly affects results.
  • Not validated in a prospective clinical study and not approved as a medical device.
  • Heatmaps show where the model looked, not why a condition is present.
06

Ethics & privacy

  • No storage: photos are processed in memory and discarded after the response.
  • No accounts or tracking: the only thing kept is an optional history of labels in your own browser.
  • Decision support only: results are meant to inform a conversation with a dermatologist, never replace one.
  • Fairness: built around Indian and South Asian skin to reduce the bias of Western-centric datasets.
  • Data use: training data was used under the terms of the respective dataset licences.
